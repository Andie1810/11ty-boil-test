# Log 1: The Silence Between Stars

{% mission 'Day 1,842' 'Location: Deep Interstellar Space, Perseus Arm (Uncharted Sector)' %}

There’s a kind of silence out here that isn’t just the absence of sound — it’s the absence of expectation. No wind. No distant engines. No hum of civilisation layered beneath everything like white noise. Just the faint vibration of the ship’s life-support systems and the occasional crackle of cosmic radiation against the hull.

I used to think loneliness would feel sharp, like a sudden cut. But it’s more like erosion. Slow. Gradual. The edges of memories wear down first — the exact sound of someone’s laugh, the way sunlight used to look through leaves, the smell of rain on concrete. I record voice logs sometimes just to hear a human cadence, even if it’s only my own.

Today I passed through a debris field — the remnants of a planetary system that never quite made it. Shattered rock, frozen gases, fragments of what might once have been moons. There was a strange comfort in it. Proof that things can fall apart completely and still remain… something. Still exist. Still occupy space.

I’ve started naming stars again. Not official catalogue names — just personal ones. There’s a blue giant I called “Home,” even though I know I’ll never go there. Naming things feels like planting flags in the infinite, tiny acts of defiance against insignificance.

People always imagine exploration as discovery. New worlds, new life, revelations. But most of it is waiting. Travelling through vast stretches of nothing, trusting equations and hope.

Still, every once in a while, the sensors ping. A new anomaly. A signal that doesn’t match known patterns. A gravitational wobble that suggests something unseen.

And in that moment, the loneliness disappears.

Because somewhere out there, something unknown exists — and I get to be the one who finds it.
