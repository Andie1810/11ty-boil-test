# Log 2: The Planet That Shouldn’t Exist

{% mission 'Day 2,019' 'Location: Orbit of Unclassified Terrestrial Planet, Outer Perseus Arm' %}

<img class="post-hero" alt="Astronaut on an alien twilight world" src="/images/blog2.png"/>

I wasn’t supposed to find a habitable world this far from any stable star.

The readings were wrong at first — they had to be. Atmospheric oxygen within survivable range. Liquid water. Temperatures hovering just below what I’d call comfortable. No detectable biosignatures beyond simple microbial life, but still… a planet capable of holding a human without a suit.

I spent six hours running diagnostics before allowing myself to believe it.

Stepping onto the surface felt unreal. After years of recycled air and synthetic gravity, the sensation of wind against my face almost made me cry. The sky is a pale violet — not dramatic, just subtly different enough to remind me I’m nowhere near Earth. The horizon stretches endlessly, broken only by low crystalline formations that hum faintly when the wind passes through them.

I took off my helmet for exactly thirty seconds at first. Training. Caution. Fear.

Then I laughed — actually laughed — because the air smelled like nothing. Clean. Neutral. Untouched by life or decay. A blank canvas planet.

I walked for kilometres without seeing any movement larger than dust.

There’s something haunting about a world that could host life but doesn’t. It feels like arriving at a party hours before anyone else, except you’re not sure anyone is coming at all.

Part of me wonders if humanity will ever reach this place. If I’m standing on soil no other human will ever touch again. The thought is both beautiful and devastating.

I collected samples, planted a beacon, and left a small object buried beneath a rock formation — a metal tag engraved with my name and the date. Proof that I existed here. That someone did.

As I lifted off, the planet shrank quickly beneath the clouds, returning to anonymity among billions of worlds.

But I know it’s there.

And for a brief moment in the history of the universe, one human stood on it and breathed its air.

Sometimes that’s enough.
